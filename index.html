      Same stock, different promises.

      B2B serves stores, distributors and business buyers. B2C serves the end consumer. Order size is a common pattern, not a strict definition: a business can also order one item.

      DimensionB2B · Store & wholesaleB2C · Consumer fulfillment
      Illustrative orderA store requests 40 cases of shampoo, sequenced for its aisles.A shopper orders one shampoo and two other products in one parcel.
      Handling unitOften full pallets, cases and mixed-case pallets.Usually eaches: individual units picked into totes.
      Order releaseScheduled replenishment; delivery appointments; route-based waves.Frequent small orders; priority by customer promise and carrier cutoff.
      PickingCase/pallet picking, store allocation and aisle sequencing.Batch/zone or goods-to-person picking; consolidate items for each order.
      Packing & dispatchStable pallets, retailer labels, advance shipment notices, freight loads.Right-size cartons, item verification, parcel labels and carrier sortation.
      Main pain pointsHeavy handling, wrong store quantities, unstable pallets, missed slots and retailer penalties.Walking, SKU confusion, promotion peaks, pack queues, returns and missed parcel cutoffs.
      Typical automation fitPallet AS/RS, autonomous forklifts, case shuttles and palletising arms.Bin AS/RS, picking AMRs, selective robotic piece picking and sorters.
      Key outcomeOn time in full (OTIF), damage rate, cost per case.Order accuracy, on-time ship rate, cost per order, return-to-stock time.
      Omnichannel challenge: a promotion and a store replenishment run can compete for the same units. Reserve inventory once, keep case-to-each conversions correct, and allocate capacity by delivery promise.

      Choose the task before the robot.

      Transport, storage and manipulation are different jobs. Technology fit depends on item geometry, order mix, peak demand and the building.

      Humanoids · Early commercial, narrow tasks

      Where a human-shaped robot could help

      A repetitive tote transfer between an existing mobile robot and conveyor is a concrete example. Human-height workstations can reduce the need to redesign every handoff.

      The same task may be better served by a fixed arm, conveyor or wheeled mobile manipulator. Compare the complete work cycle, recovery effort and operating cost.

      What the evidence does not establish

      • Reliable handling of every SKU, deformable bag, damaged carton or unexpected obstacle.
      • Site-specific uptime, intervention rate, battery autonomy or cost per successful transfer.
      • Safe unrestricted work beside people. The exact application needs its own assessed safeguards.

      Pilot gate: use real items and peak duty cycles; log successful transfers, drops, interventions, recovery time, availability and total cost. Compare against a simpler automation baseline.

      The software makes the machines a warehouse.

      This is a reference design, not a mandatory vendor stack. Some products combine roles. The essential rule is clear ownership of orders, stock, task execution and machine safety.

      Commerce / ERP / OMSCustomer promise & allocationStores, marketplaces and web orders arrive here. The order management system (OMS) routes demand and reserves availability; enterprise resource planning (ERP) manages commercial records.
      ↓ Order release and allocation · ↑ Shipment status and availability
      WMSWarehouse management systemOwns warehouse inventory locations and stock movements. Directs receiving, putaway, replenishment, picking and shipping. A scanned or confirmed movement updates the inventory ledger.
      ↓ Executable work · ↑ Completed, short-picked or failed
      WES / WCS / Fleet managersCoordinate executionA warehouse execution system (WES) balances queues and workstations; a warehouse control system (WCS) manages equipment flow; fleet managers dispatch robots. Define who sequences each resource.
      ↓ Machine commands · ↑ Position, health and completion events
      Robot controllers / PLCsPhysical execution & safetyMotion, sensors and local control execute the task. Safety-rated controls enforce stops and interlocks independently of generative AI and cloud availability.

      Where AI is useful

      • Forecast order demand and recommend replenishment or slotting.
      • Use vision to detect labels, item identity or possible damage within validated limits.
      • Suggest labor and queue adjustments; explain exceptions to supervisors.
      • Flag equipment anomalies from telemetry for maintenance review.

      These are application patterns; benefit and model accuracy need site validation.

      What must work before AI

      • Correct SKU dimensions, weights, case sizes, barcodes and locations.
      • One authoritative inventory record and reliable task acknowledgements.
      • Duplicate-safe events, retries and reconciliation after a network outage.
      • Explicit human recovery for short picks, damaged stock, jams and robot failures.
